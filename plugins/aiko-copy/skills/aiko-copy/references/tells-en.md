# Tells of AI-written English

Look for clusters, not single words. One "robust" is fine; five buzzwords in a paragraph are not.
Tells change with every model generation, so the principle behind each line matters more than the word.
Not tells: correct grammar, a formal register, transition words on their own.

## Words
Clusters of: delve, underscore, showcase, pivotal, intricate, meticulous, tapestry, testament,
seamless, robust, leverage, elevate, unlock, foster, crucial, vibrant, boasts, landscape, realm,
comprehensive, additionally, notably, transformative, game-changer.
Studies of millions of texts show these jumped after 2023 ("delves" about 28×). Most are verbs.

- Nominalizations: "promoting sustainable consumption patterns" → "helping people buy less".
- Elegant variation: "the platform… the tool… the system" for one thing → repeat the name, or "it".

## Constructions
| Tell | Example | Rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| Not X but Y | "It's not a tool — it's a mindset." | "It changes how you plan." |
| Not just / not only | "Not just a CMS, but a home for ideas." | "A CMS for a personal site." |
| Not because… Because… | "Not because it's fast. Because it's honest." | Give the reason once. |
| Avoiding "is" | "The page serves as a hub for settings." | "Settings live here." |
| Significance tail | "…in 2019, marking a pivotal moment for the team." | End at "2019". |
| Inflated role | "Caching plays a crucial role in speed." | "Without the cache, pages take 3 s." |
| Rule of three by habit | "fast, reliable, and secure" | Name the one that is true and matters. |
| Staccato drama | "Three words. That's it. That's the product." | One normal sentence. |
| Q then A | "The result? Faster builds." | "Builds are faster: 40 s instead of 90." |
| Throat-clearing | "Here's the thing:", "Let me be clear:", "At its core," | Start with the point. |
| Didactic hedge | "It's important to note that…" | Delete the lead-in. |
| Vague authority | "Experts argue…" | Name the source or drop it. |
| False breadth | "Whether you're a beginner or a pro…" | Name the real reader. |
| Generic ending | "Despite challenges, X continues to thrive." | End on the last fact. |

## Structure and formatting
- Headings, bold and bullets on a three-sentence answer.
- "**Speed:** Pages load faster." bullets where a paragraph would do.
- Title Case Headings. Emoji on headings or bullets.
- A closing summary that repeats the text. "In summary", "Key takeaways".
- Uniform rhythm: every sentence 15–20 words, every paragraph 3 sentences. This tell survives longest.
- Em dashes above ~3 per 1000 words. Humans use them too; the density gives it away.

## Tone
- Sycophancy: "Great question!", "You're absolutely right!"
- Announcing instead of saying: "Let's dive in", "Here's a breakdown:".
- Closing offers: "I hope this helps", "Let me know if…", "Would you like me to…".
- Promotional tone on neutral topics; certainty with no stance, no example, no number.
- Knowledge hedges: "based on available information".

## UI
- Benefit-speak: "Unlock your potential", "Seamlessly manage…" → "Save", "Add palette".
- Excited status: "Awesome! You're all set! 🎉" → "Saved".
- "Oops! Something went wrong 😅" → "Couldn't save. Check your connection and try again."
