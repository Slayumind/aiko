# Domains and the process

## The process proposal

Write it before the domains, short, for one reader who decides (often the head of the team). It
answers:

- **Who owns the shared backlog** on the requesting side: one person, who sets priorities there.
  Prioritising is the owner's job; without it the owner is not an owner.
- **Who has a veto** and who solves conflicts over resources. Escalation goes to them on our side,
  and to the leaders of the other teams on theirs.
- **Domain owners**: each keeps their domain current and proofreads what agents prepared. One
  backlog owner cannot read the context of every task; a domain owner can.
- **What agents do**, as a separate block: read the chats and trackers, rewrite requests from other
  teams into the backlog format, move statuses, rebuild the roadmap once a week. Owners check the
  agents' work and answer for it.
- **The rhythm**: owners update their sources during the week; agents rebuild the roadmap; owners
  read it **before** the meeting; the meeting uses the roadmap as one source of the agenda.

## A domain card

| Field | What goes there |
|---|---|
| Name | in English, the same in every place |
| Owner | one person; "not named yet" is allowed and visible |
| About | one sentence: what the domain changes for people |
| Borders | In and Out, short lists |
| Sources | where the owner keeps things: channels, backlog, docs |
| Labels | existing tracker labels that find the domain's tasks; "none" is allowed |
| Goals | the quarter's goals the domain works for |

## Domain pages

If a good example exists in the company (one owner already wrote clear pages), use its shape.
Two pages per domain worked well:

**The domain page**

1. Business effect: one sentence.
2. Borders: In / Out of scope.
3. Target metrics for the quarter: baseline → target, deadline, type (primary, secondary,
   guardrail), whose goal.
4. Team and resources: role, person, load. Unknown: "[TBC]".
5. What we're doing: by stream.
6. Releases: the backlog with one row per release (see `releases.md`).
7. Dependencies and risks: open blockers, who answers.
8. Sources: where colleagues and agents read statuses.

**How the work runs** (a child page)

Roles; what the team works for; the week's rhythm (with the shared sync day); how requests come in
(channel, form); where tasks are kept and how results are reported; what to do when something
breaks.

Write both pages in plain English. Show them to the user before creating them. After the user
edits them by hand, the shared pages become the main copy: fetch them before any later sync.
